T–90

The show is won in the advance.

Site plans, sightlines and a load-in sequence that survives contact with a real dock. Riders read, tech packs reconciled, every truck given a slot before one is booked. By the time steel arrives, the arguments are already over.

Trucks hit the yard in the dark.

Routing, marshalling and dock control — so trailers land in the order the build needs them, not the order they showed up. The yard is the first place a schedule goes wrong, and the cheapest place to fix it.

Load-in day · yard control, marshalling

Steel up, power on.

Main stage, sub-stages, risers and roof. Generators, distro and a tie-in with enough redundancy that nothing on the bill ever learns there was a problem. Crew calls sized to the build, stewarded, and pushed to the schedule.

Doors. Now none of it can move.

Green rooms stocked, credentials issued, travel landed, changeovers rehearsed. From here the day runs on comms and the plan holding — twenty-minute turns, no second attempt, and a public who should never know how close anything ran.

Gone before the site wakes up.

Stage down under work light, trucks loaded in reverse of the plan that put them there, ground handed back the way we found it. Load-out is the part clients remember when they're deciding who to call next year.
